Steam is the largest PC game store. Players vote tags onto each game; the top tags drive store search.

1The decision

An indie studio's creative director picks the two-tag concept for the next game. With no pair passing the rule in section 5, they would take a proven, crowded pair, e.g. {CA} × {CB}.

2Why a network

Counts can't tell whether two tags attract different players. The network can: tags sharing few neighbours draw on audiences that rarely meet, so a game combining them can reach both.

4Graph construction

NodeA Steam tag. Undirected, weighted tag–tag projection of the game–tag bipartite graph.
EdgeCo-occurrence in a game's top 5 tags; suggests relatedness, not a stated relation.
ResolutionNone: Steam tags come from a fixed list.
WeightNPMI: co-occurrence vs chance, −1 to 1 (0 = chance). By raw count, {raw_touch} of the top-10 edges touch the 5 commonest tags; by NPMI, {npmi_touch}.
WindowTop 5, as the store page shows. With all 20, the pair's reviews vs the median game fall from {lift}× to {k20_lift}× ({k20_n} games, mostly minor tags).
Excluded“Indie” (seed); release-model, adult tags; tags on >25% of games: {generic}.
PruningFlat cutoffs: tag on ≥1.5% of games ({min_tag}); edge NPMI ≥ 0.10 and ≥0.5% shared ({min_co}); pair ≥0.8% ({min_pair}).

5The picture

Selection rule: among pairs on ≥{min_pair} games, keep those not crowded (games ≤ 1.5× chance, where chance = games with A × games with B ÷ {pop}) whose tags sit in separate neighbourhoods (≤20% shared neighbours); pick the one whose median reviews are highest relative to the median game ({cmed}).

  • {top10_crowded} of the 10 highest-review pairs are crowded (>1.5× chance), e.g. {CA} × {CB} ({csat}×).
  • Robust: #1 at all {cut_n} edge cutoffs; beats the typical game in {boot_gt1}/100 resamples and on S2. Weak spot: #1 in only {top1}/100 resamples, as it sits right at the {min_pair}-game minimum.
