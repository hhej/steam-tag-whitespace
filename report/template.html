Steam is the largest PC game store. Players vote tags onto each game; the top tags drive store search.

1The decision

An indie studio's creative director picks the next game's two lead tags; if no pair passed the selection rule, they would pick a crowded pair (table).

2Why a network

Counts show how common a pair is and how well it sells, not whether its tags belong to separate groups or which tag connects them. That needs the network.

4Graph construction

NodeA Steam tag. Undirected, weighted tag–tag projection of the game–tag bipartite graph.
EdgeCo-occurrence in a game's top 5 tags; suggests relatedness, not a stated relation.
ResolutionCo-op, Online/Local Co-Op and Local Multiplayer merged into Multiplayer ({coop} of Horror × Multiplayer games were also tagged co-op).
WeightNPMI (co-occurrence above expected). Raw counts give {raw_touch} of the top 10 edges to the 5 most common tags; NPMI gives none.
WindowTop 5 tags per game, as the store shows; with all 20, #1 falls from {lift}× to {k20_lift}×.
Excluded“Indie”, Early Access, Free to Play, adult; on >25% of games: {generic}.
PruningTag on ≥1.5% of games ({min_tag}); edge needs NPMI ≥ 0.10 and ≥0.5% of games ({min_co}) with both tags. {tags_before} tags → {nodes} nodes, {edges} edges.

5The picture

Expected = games with both tags if picked independently (A's games × B's games ÷ {pop}); crowding = games ÷ expected; positive reviews = thumbs-up reviews of the pair's typical game. Rule: of pairs on ≥{min_pair} games, keep crowding ≤ 1.5 and ≤20% of neighbours shared; rank by positive reviews.
