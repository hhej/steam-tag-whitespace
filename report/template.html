Steam is the largest PC game store. Players vote tags onto each game; the top tags drive store search.

1The decision

An indie studio's creative director picks the next game's two lead tags; if no pair passed the selection rule, they would pick a crowded one (table, last row).

2Why a network

Counts give crowding and reviews. Which uncrowded pairs join separate neighbourhoods, and through which tag, needs neighbours and paths, which counts lack.

4Graph construction

NodeA Steam tag. Undirected, weighted tag–tag projection of the game–tag bipartite graph.
EdgeCo-occurrence in a game's top 5 tags; suggests relatedness, not a stated relation.
ResolutionCo-op, Online/Local Co-Op, Local Multiplayer → Multiplayer ({coop} of Horror × Multiplayer games were co-op too).
WeightNPMI: co-occurrence vs chance (0 = chance). By raw count, {raw_touch} of the top-10 edges touch the 5 commonest tags; by NPMI, {npmi_touch}.
WindowTop 5 tags per game, as the store page shows; with all 20, #1's reviews drop from {lift}× to {k20_lift}×.
Excluded“Indie”; Early Access, Free to Play, adult tags; on >25% of games: {generic}.
PruningTag on ≥1.5% of games ({min_tag}); edge needs NPMI ≥ 0.10 and ≥0.5% of games ({min_co}) with both tags. {tags_before} tags → {nodes} nodes, {edges} edges.

5The picture

Selection rule: of pairs on ≥{min_pair} games (0.8%), keep those not crowded (games ≤ 1.5× chance; chance = games with A × games with B ÷ {pop}) with ≤20% shared neighbours; rank by median reviews ÷ {cmed}.
