1The decision

The creative director of an indie studio decides which two-tag game concept to build next. If no pair had passed the test below, they would pick the best-performing crowded pair, {FA} × {FB}.

2Why a network

Tag counts show which tags are common. They cannot show which two tags sit in separate parts of the tag network yet do well when combined; that needs each tag's neighbours.

3Data

6Findings

  • {broker} has the highest betweenness ({bc}) and links {broker_deg} tags from different parts of the network.
  • {A} × {B}: {n_ab} games (expected {exp}), no edge between them, neighbour overlap {ov}. Their median game has {med} reviews, {lift}× the cohort median of {cmed} (permutation p = {perm}).
  • {FA} × {FB} has the highest lift ({flift}×) but already has {fsat}× more games than expected. {A} × {B} stays #1 at all {cut_n} pruning cutoffs and on S1 tags. Lift is above 1 in {boot_gt1}/100 bootstraps, but the pair is #1 in only {top1}/100 because only {n_ab} games support it.

7Recommended action

  • Pitch {A} × {B} at the next greenlight meeting, with {FA} × {FB} as plan B.
  • Build a prototype and open a Steam Coming Soon page with {A} and {B} as its first two tags.
  • Re-run this analysis on 2025 releases before full production. If the pair drops out of the top 3, switch to plan B.

8What would change my mind

Only {n_ab} games support the pair, so one hit game could explain the lift. If the pair drops out of the top 3 on 2025 releases, I switch to {FA} × {FB}.
