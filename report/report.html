Which tag pair should an indie game use? First-Person × Multiplayer

MADT 7202 Mid-Term · Panjapol Ampornratana 6810424009 · Repo: github.com/hhej/steam-tag-whitespace

Steam is the largest PC game store. Players vote tags onto each game; the top tags drive store search.

1The decision

An indie studio's creative director chooses the next game's two tags. If no pair passed the rule in section 5, they would take a crowded pair (table, last row).

2Why a network

Counts can't show whether two tags draw different players. The network can: tags with few shared neighbours point to audiences that rarely meet.

4Graph construction

NodeA Steam tag. Undirected, weighted tag–tag projection of the game–tag bipartite graph.
EdgeCo-occurrence in a game's top 5 tags; suggests relatedness, not a stated relation.
ResolutionCo-op, Online/Local Co-Op, Local Multiplayer → Multiplayer (84% of Horror × Multiplayer games were co-op too).
WeightNPMI: co-occurrence vs chance (0 = chance). By raw count, 9 of the top-10 edges touch the 5 commonest tags; by NPMI, 0.
WindowTop 5 tags per game (as on the store page). With all 20, #1 spans 375 games and its reviews drop from 5.1× to 3.0×.
Excluded“Indie”; Early Access, Free to Play, adult; on >25% of games: Singleplayer, Adventure, Action, Casual, 2D, 3D, Atmospheric, Exploration.
PruningTag on ≥1.5% of games (136); edge needs NPMI ≥ 0.10 and ≥0.5% of games (45) with both tags. 439 tags → 80 nodes, 177 edges.

5The picture

Selection rule: among pairs on ≥73 games, keep those not crowded (games ≤ 1.5× chance; chance = games with A × games with B ÷ 9,094) with tags in separate neighbourhoods (≤20% shared neighbours); rank by median reviews vs the median game (40).

6Findings

  • #1 First-Person × Multiplayer is near chance (NPMI 0.02, no edge) yet gets 5.1× the median reviews (permutation p < 0.001); 68% are horror.
  • Both tests bite: 8 of the top 10 pairs by reviews are crowded; the network test drops 3 of 35 uncrowded pairs (e.g. RPG × Visual Novel).
  • Holds up: #1 at all 8 edge cutoffs; beats the median in 100/100 bootstraps; 3.4× without its top 5 games. Weak spot: #1 in 59/100.

7Recommended action

  • Week 0: pitch a first-person multiplayer horror game (#1); plan B is #2.
  • Weeks 1–6: prototype. Week 7: Coming Soon page, tagged First-Person and Multiplayer first.
  • Before full production: re-run on 2025 data (section 8).

8What would change my mind

#1 and #2 are both multiplayer horror; "not crowded" is vs chance, not vs the 2023–24 co-op horror boom. If 2025 releases cross 1.5×, I switch to #3.
